# Collection-2

Noir Street — Found-Footage Short Production Instructions

Purpose of the camera
This is a continuation of Upload 003's exploration following his discovery of the Staging Plane and entry into the hotel. The camera is the same first-person capture camcorder used throughout his earlier recordings.
The recording begins with him already standing inside the Noir Street scenario. Do not show him opening a hotel door, crossing a threshold, or entering the scenario at the beginning. The audience should feel that the recording has resumed in the middle of his ongoing journey.
He has no assigned task and no reliable extraction procedure. He is exploring the environment, trying to understand why it exists, and looking for a way out of the CSN.
The camera must remain an in-universe recording device, not a filmmaking camera. Its movement, framing, autofocus, exposure, and audio should behave like a handheld consumer camcorder operated by someone walking through an unfamiliar environment.
Act 1: The empty Noir Street
* Open on Upload 003 already standing in the middle of an empty city street. The recording is active, with a visible October 23, 2025 date stamp.
* Establish the environment through a slow, natural look around. Tall period buildings, wet pavement, old streetlamps, dark shopfronts, vintage cars, fire escapes, and distant architectural silhouettes establish a stylized 1930s to 1940s noir atmosphere.
* The visual reference is the moody, period-inspired noir city aesthetic of Shadows of Evil from Call of Duty: Black Ops III. Use this as an aesthetic reference only. Do not reproduce its specific characters, signage, logos, or recognizable locations.
* The street is completely empty. There are no pedestrians, moving cars, voices, or visible creatures.
* He walks down the street, looking into shop windows and occasionally turning to inspect buildings. His footsteps echo against the hard surfaces. The city feels physically substantial, not like a flat painted backdrop.
* He comments naturally: "This area looks interesting. I wonder what this scenario is planned for."
* After a short pause, he adds: "I really need to find a way to extract. I should not be here."
* The dialogue should sound like private thoughts spoken aloud by someone who has been stranded for months. He is curious about the environment but increasingly frustrated by his inability to leave.
Act 2: The hat shop
* He discovers an old-fashioned hat shop and enters through its front door.
* The shop should contain wooden display cabinets, dusty glass counters, hat stands, shelves, period wallpaper, worn flooring, and several examples of vintage headwear. Use believable materials and detailed textures, with subtle signs of age.
* The interior is silent apart from his footsteps, clothing rustle, breathing, and the faint ambient noise of the city.
* He moves behind the counter or around the displays, examining the shop as someone trying to understand what kind of scenario has been constructed.
* He notices a top hat and picks it up. Hold the camera on it long enough for the audience to register the shape, fabric, worn edges, and realistic material.
* The interaction must feel casual and exploratory, not like a dramatic discovery. He is interested in the object, but he does not suddenly understand its significance.
* Preserve the established character continuity: Upload 003 is a test subject, and the hat is connected to his original identity. Do not invent an explanation for why this particular shop contains the hat, and do not introduce another character or an apparition.
* After examining the hat, he puts it down or carries it according to what produces the most believable continuity with his established appearance. Do not accidentally imply that he has become a different person or that this is a second consciousness.
* He eventually leaves the shop and continues along the street.
Act 3: The abandoned theater
* He discovers an old, abandoned theater and enters.
* Establish a convincing period interior: a faded marquee or entrance sign, worn carpet, dark wood, aging decorative plaster, rows of empty seats, a stage, and a large projector screen. The theater should feel abandoned rather than destroyed.
* He explores the auditorium, briefly looking across the seats and toward the stage. The scale should feel larger than the shop, with the camera capturing only what a person would naturally notice while walking through it.
* There must be no audience, actors, projected film, or other living presence.
* The room is quiet enough that individual footsteps, the camera's handling noise, and distant building ambience are audible.
* He begins arranging nine available objects into a ring on the theater floor. Choose objects that plausibly belong in the environment, such as small theater accessories or movable items found near the seating area. Do not introduce obviously artificial puzzle pieces.
* The objects must be evenly spaced around an imaginary circle, leaving exactly one of the ten positions empty.
* He steps into the missing position himself, completing the ten-position arrangement. The empty position must face the camera's direction before he occupies it. This is the established glyph pattern: nine objects arranged around a ring with one missing position, where the tenth position belongs to the person.
* The objects rotate and spin in place erratically and sink into the floor, with an audible two-note chime signaling that this is unexpected and not accounted to happen. This is the recurring audio cue.
* The objects begin to rotate or move subtly, then sink into the floor. Keep the effect brief and physically unsettling, rather than spectacular. The two-note chime is the important confirmation that the glyph event has occurred.
* At the same moment, the theater projector screen briefly flickers with a deep red illumination. The red should appear as light interacting with the screen and nearby surfaces, not as a flat digital overlay.
* The projector should not suddenly display an image of a monster, a face, or explanatory text. The audience should not receive a direct explanation of what has happened.
* The red flicker fades, and the theater returns to its previous appearance. Leave a brief moment of stillness so the audience can process the event.
* Upload 003 does not celebrate or explain the event. He has learned to use the glyph, and his behavior should suggest that he is deliberately repeating a known procedure rather than discovering it for the first time.
Ending: October 25, 2025
* The recording must communicate that two days have passed since October 23. Use the camcorder date display, environmental lighting, and the character's behavior to establish the passage of time naturally. Do not insert an explanatory montage or a title card that breaks the found-footage format.
* The two-day duration is important to the larger chronology. The scenario should feel like another stretch of time spent searching for an exit, not a short excursion completed immediately.
* He leaves the theater and continues through the empty street, returning to the doorway or transition point that connects this scenario to the hotel.
* The whole short is in first person. The return must be shown in first person. The camera approaches the doorway, crosses the threshold, and reveals the familiar hotel corridor on the other side.
* The hotel corridor must match the established environment, especially its carpet pattern, lighting, and architecture. Do not redesign the hotel or turn the corridor into another horror environment.
* The camera continues a short distance into the corridor. The doorway remains visible long enough to establish that he has genuinely returned to the hotel, rather than simply reaching another room in the Noir Street scenario.
* End the recording while he is in the hotel corridor. Do not show him entering another scenario, returning to the Staging Plane, or beginning the next short. The next recording will continue his exploration of the hotel and its connected scenarios.

Noir Street: Style Prompt
A single first-person handheld camcorder recording from inside an abandoned noir city simulation, created as part of a company’s Consciousness Simulation Network. The recording begins with Upload 003 already standing inside the scenario and ends when he walks through a doorway back into the familiar hotel corridor. Genre reference for visual atmosphere only: period noir crime cinema, the moody 1930s to 1940s-inspired city aesthetic of Shadows of Evil, and quiet found-footage psychological horror. Never use any existing organisation, logo, game title, or film title inside the film. All in-universe signage, objects, and environmental details must be original.
1. Essence and what it is not
* One first-person handheld recording with a 4:3 VHS-era camcorder appearance.
* The city is empty, period-inspired, atmospheric, and physically believable.
* The story progresses through exploration: the street, the hat shop, the theater, and the return to the hotel.
* The unsettling moment is the glyph ritual inside the theater, accompanied by the familiar two-note chime and a brief red flicker on the projector screen.
* The environment should not transform into a different world or become an action sequence.
* No monsters, visible pursuers, gore, jump scares, or conventional horror-movie music.
* The Man should remain a believable, frustrated consciousness trying to escape an environment in which he was never supposed to remain.
* The scenario is a simulation, not an alternate universe or a parallel reality. Do not add dialogue explaining this.
2. Story shape
Time	Beat
0:00 to 0:30	Open with him already standing in the empty Noir Street scenario. Establish the period architecture, wet pavement, streetlamps, and empty road. He comments that the area looks interesting and wonders what the scenario is planned for.
0:30 to 0:55	He expresses his need to find a way to extract and says he should not be there. He walks into the hat shop and examines the interior.
0:55 to 1:15	He notices and picks up a top hat, inspects it briefly, and leaves the shop. Keep the interaction mundane and natural.
1:15 to 1:40	He discovers and explores the abandoned theater. The camera surveys the empty auditorium, seats, stage, and projector screen.
1:40 to 2:05	He arranges nine objects in a ring, occupies the missing tenth position, and performs the glyph ritual. The two-note chime sounds, the objects move and sink, and the projector screen briefly flickers red.
2:05 to 2:30	Establish the passage of two days, with the date reaching October 25, 2025. He returns through the connected doorway, enters the hotel corridor, and says, "Finally. I made my way back to the hotel." End in the corridor.
The timing is a guide, not a reason to rush the dialogue or leave unnatural gaps. Allow the camera to linger when the environment merits attention. If the finished short needs to run slightly longer to preserve believable movement and speech, prioritize the quality of the recording over rigid timing.
3. Materials and rendering
* Frame: 4:3 image inside a 1920×1080 output. Render the world at a lower resolution, such as 720×540, and upscale through the camcorder processing.
* Build the street as a walkable 3D environment with convincing depth, realistic perspective, and enough geometry to support natural head turns and walking.
* Create a coherent street layout with buildings, sidewalks, shop entrances, period streetlights, parked cars, fire escapes, and distant architectural silhouettes.
* The buildings should suggest an early-to-mid twentieth-century American city. Use brick, stone, painted timber, old glass, iron railings, and weathered plaster.
* Wet pavement should have subtle reflections of streetlights and windows. Avoid a mirror-like surface or excessive rain effects.
* The hat shop should contain textured wooden cabinets, glass display cases, shelves, hat stands, period wallpaper, and a worn floor. The top hat must be a real 3D object that can be approached and picked up.
* The theater should have believable seating rows, an aisle, a stage, a large projector screen, architectural ornament, aged carpet, and worn surfaces.
* Implement the glyph using nine physical objects that visibly rotate or descend into the floor. The objects must be consistent throughout the sequence and must not pop into or out of existence without a visible transition.
* The red projector flicker must illuminate the screen and cast a small amount of reflected light into the auditorium.
* The hotel corridor must be a separate, recognizable environment connected through a real doorway or transition boundary. Preserve the existing hotel carpet pattern and corridor design.
* The entire project must be contained in one self-contained HTML file. Embed the code, procedural assets, generated textures, and any permitted external assets locally within the file. Do not require a server, external runtime, or network connection to run the finished film.
* When appropriate, find high-resolution textures online for brick, plaster, wet pavement, aged wood, carpet, and theater materials. Use assets only when their licensing permits the intended use, and embed the resulting files directly in the HTML. If external textures cannot be embedded, generate convincing procedural alternatives.
4. Color logic
* Street: Muted charcoal, soot-black, aged brown brick, desaturated blue-gray shadows, and warm amber streetlamps. The city should feel nocturnal without crushing every detail into black.
* Shop: Warm, dirty amber practical lighting against dark wood, dusty glass, and faded wallpaper. The top hat should remain clearly readable.
* Theater: Deep burgundy, dark brown, aged gold ornament, and subdued warm lighting. The auditorium should have sufficient visible detail to read as a real space.
* Glyph event: The brief red flicker is the only strong color disruption. Use deep, dark red rather than bright alarm red. The screen should bloom slightly, and nearby surfaces should receive believable reflected illumination.
* Hotel: Return to the established corridor's exact lighting and carpet appearance. The transition should be immediately recognizable through continuity rather than an explanatory line.
* Do not use a uniform color filter as a substitute for physically grounded lighting. Allow the camcorder's automatic white balance and exposure to respond imperfectly to changes in illumination.
5. Type and subtitles
* Use the same camcorder OSD style established in the other recordings: a bitmap-style font, REC indicator, battery display, timestamp, and any established recording indicators.
* The opening date must read October 23, 2025. The recording must later show October 25, 2025.
* Do not display a conventional cinematic title card at the beginning. The recording is already in progress when the short starts.
* Use the project's established subtitle style: clean, readable, Netflix-style captions with natural sentence grouping. Avoid excessive fragmentation and allow enough time for each complete line to be read.
* Captions should remain stable and readable above the OSD, without being distorted by the VHS effect.
* Suggested dialogue:
    * "This area looks interesting."
    * "I wonder what this scenario is planned for."
    * "I really need to find a way to extract."
    * "I should not be here."
    * "Finally. I made my way back to the hotel."
* Do not force additional dialogue into quiet moments. The recording should communicate most of its atmosphere through movement, environmental sound, and visual evidence.
6. Motion quality
* The camera is handheld at chest height, slightly low and wide, with small walking-induced vertical movement and natural side-to-side sway.
* Use small, imperfect adjustments as he looks at storefronts, turns toward the hat shop, examines the top hat, surveys the theater, and looks down at the glyph arrangement.
* When he picks up the hat, the camera should tilt and move in a way that plausibly corresponds to a person reaching for an object. Do not show impossible third-person views of his face or body.
* The camera should remain steady enough for the audience to inspect the environment, with occasional autofocus hunting and exposure lag.
* Walking speed should be human and consistent with the character's cautious exploration.
* The glyph objects must move with a deliberate, coherent animation. They should rotate or shift, descend into the floor, and trigger the established audio cue.
* The red projector flicker should be brief and synchronized with the ritual. Do not fill the frame with glitch effects.
* The return to the hotel should be a continuous, physically understandable movement through the doorway. The corridor should appear on the other side of the threshold with consistent perspective and scale.
* The final camera movement should slow once he reaches the corridor, suggesting relief without becoming theatrical.
7. Camera grammar

Nothing should be framed like a conventional horror reveal. The camera is recording what the operator sees because he is navigating the environment, not because he is trying to frighten an audience.
8. Sound palette
* No conventional music score. Sound should come from the environment and the recording equipment.
* Street: Footsteps on wet pavement, faint wind through the buildings, subtle electrical hum from streetlamps, distant indistinct urban ambience, clothing rustle, and breathing.
* Hat shop: Footsteps changing from pavement to wood or tile, a quiet door creak, faint glass and wood contact when he handles the hat, and subdued indoor room tone.
* Theater: Footsteps across worn flooring and carpet, slight reverberation in the auditorium, soft clothing movement, and a distant electrical hum from the projector equipment.
* Glyph: The recurring two-note chime must match the sound used in the origin recording. Follow it with restrained object movement, low mechanical resonance, or a subtle descending sound as the objects sink.
* Red flicker: A brief projector click or electrical flutter may accompany the red illumination, but avoid a loud sting or dramatic impact.
* Hotel corridor: Footsteps should change to match the hotel's carpet. The familiar corridor ambience should replace the city's open-air sound.
* Mix: Preserve dialogue clarity and dynamic range. Avoid heavy reverb, over-compressed ambience, or artificial bass intended to make every moment frightening.
9. Native moves
* Environmental storytelling: The period architecture, shop displays, and abandoned theater establish the scenario without exposition.
* Object continuity: The same top hat must remain consistent between the shop interaction and any subsequent shots in which it is visible.
* Recurring glyph: The nine-object ring with one missing position is the established Reintegration Artifact pattern. Its use should connect this recording to the broader Man storyline.
* Recurring chime: The two-note cue connects the ritual to the earlier recordings and rewards viewers who recognize it.
* Red as evidence: The brief red flicker should suggest that the ritual has affected the scenario without confirming exactly what the effect is.
* Time as evidence: The date changing from October 23 to October 25 establishes the two-day duration. Do not make the passage of time depend solely on dialogue.
* Hotel continuity: The hotel is the persistent environment linking the different scenario recordings. Its return must be visually consistent with earlier and later hotel footage.
10. Pitfalls
* Do not begin with him entering through a doorway. The opening is already inside the Noir Street scenario.
* Do not end inside the theater or on the street. He must physically walk through the connecting doorway back into the hotel corridor.
* Do not make the two-day duration appear to be a few minutes. Establish the date change clearly.
* Do not populate the city with crowds, moving vehicles, or visible creatures. The emptiness is essential.
* Do not turn the hat shop into a supernatural set piece. The hat interaction is mundane and understated.
* Do not make the glyph ritual look like a magical combat ability. It is a procedure he has learned through repeated experimentation.
* Do not change the glyph's structure. It must have nine objects, ten positions, and the person occupying the missing position.
* Do not omit the two-note chime or replace it with a different sound.
* Do not turn the red projector flicker into a sustained red-world transformation. This is a brief event.
* Do not explain the purpose of the scenario or reveal why the theater reacts.
* Do not make the company explicitly evil through dialogue, logos, or exposition.
* Do not use clean, glossy CGI surfaces everywhere. The city must feel physically constructed, aged, and believable through the camcorder.
* Do not use excessive VHS glitches, scanlines, or digital corruption to manufacture atmosphere.
* Do not introduce new lore about the hotel, the hat, or the Staging Plane that conflicts with the established continuity.
* Do not show the Man's face from a third-person perspective. The entire recording stays in first person.

* shop.js: Hat shop interior, display cases, shelves, lighting, and the interactive top hat.
* theater.js: Auditorium geometry, seating, stage, projector screen, and glyph-object arrangement.

When exporting the video as an MP4, provide separate checkboxes to optionally exclude Music, Sound Effects, and Voice/Narration, allowing each audio element to be exported independently. The absence of music should be the default for this short.

13. Realism
The goal is footage that looks captured, not rendered. When realism conflicts with polish, choose the less polished, more physically plausible option.
Camera behavior
* Use a consumer camcorder with a small CCD sensor and imperfect electronic stabilization.
* Keep the camera at chest height, slightly low and wide.
* Add restrained rolling-shutter skew on fast turns and motion blur only during quick movement.
* Let autofocus hunt on low-contrast surfaces and recover imperfectly.
* Allow exposure and white balance to lag when moving between streetlights, the shop interior, the theater, and the red flicker.
Physically grounded environments
* Streetlights should illuminate nearby brick, pavement, and parked vehicles rather than producing uniform pools of light.
* Wet surfaces should reflect light unevenly according to their roughness.
* The shop and theater should have believable shadow depth and material variation.
* The projector flicker must affect the scene through actual illumination where possible.
* The hotel doorway must connect to the corridor with consistent scale and perspective.
The operator as a person
* Footsteps and camera movement should remain synchronized.
* Dialogue should be delivered while walking or pausing naturally, not like a scripted monologue.
* His curiosity should coexist with his frustration. He is trying to understand the environment because he needs to escape it.
* Do not make him react with exaggerated fear to the glyph event. He understands the ritual and is using it deliberately.
Realism do-not list
* No cinematic third-person shots.
* No impossible camera angles or perfectly smooth movement.
* No visible body or face of the operator.
* No artificial visual effects that are not motivated by the environment.
* No constant glitches or exaggerated tape damage.
* No visible monster, pursuer, or unexplained silhouette.
* No explanatory ending card replacing the required return to the hotel.
Approximate the look of real period locations captured on a 1990s consumer camcorder: soft detail, crushed shadows, noisy dark areas, blooming practical lights, subtle chroma smearing, imperfect autofocus, and occasional exposure recovery.
Final delivery requirement: Produce the complete, working, self-contained HTML player and verify that the entire short plays from beginning to end. Check the date progression, glyph-object count, recurring chime, red projector flicker, and final transition into the hotel corridor before delivery.

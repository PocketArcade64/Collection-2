# Collection-2

Subway Platform Build Instructions

Aim for a liminal feel like the pool short and just like the pool short, show exploring a bit to establish the vastness and liminal nature of the subway tunnels.

0. Read this first (you have no prior context)
You are building a single, self-contained HTML file that renders and exports a 2:50 first-person found-footage short. All assets (textures, audio, fonts, code) must be embedded inside the one HTML file (base64 data URIs) so it works when sent to another computer with no internet. Where textures are needed, find HD, freely licensed textures online (Poly Haven and ambientCG are CC0), downscale to 1K or 2K, compress, and embed them. Never use any real organisation, brand, logo, game or film title inside the film.
The universe in brief
* The Company runs the CSN (Consciousness Simulation Network). A person (the Source Subject) is photographed, then lies on a procedure table while the CSN copies their consciousness into a Cognitive Instance (CI). The CI is loaded into a simulated environment (a Build), runs a training scenario, then its experience is compressed into a Delta and written back into the Source Subject's body (Reintegration). The CI is then retired. The Company is calm, professional and believes this is beneficial. Nothing is ever explained to the audience.
* Upload 003 was one of the first three uploads. During a simple orientation puzzle the connection dropped. The extraction protocol had no handling for an unresponsive copy, so the original body woke up fine, the file was marked closed, and the copy was never pulled out. That copy is the Hat Man, and he is this film's protagonist and camera operator. He has been inside the CSN since June 10, 2025, with no evaluator and no objective, and he has learned how the simulation works by walking its edges.
* The glyph: the official extraction mark is ten dots in a ring. Every manual extraction sign carries it. The Hat Man's power comes from it: he arranges nine identical objects in an evenly spaced ring and stands in the gap as the tenth position. The objects rotate, spin erratically and sink into the floor, a two-note chime sounds, and a red glow comes from his hands. This chime is the recurring audio cue of the series.
* The Staging Plane: a blank, completely white environment that every CI passes through between Builds. It is unmonitored. It contains many door-shaped holes in the white, each tagged with a Build designation. On October 23, 2025 he planted his glyph in every door frame there, so every door now has a faint red glow at its edges.
* The Hotel: an endless hotel corridor he reaches from the Staging Plane (the door tagged HOTEL). Each hotel room door leads to a different Build. The doors, in the order he enters them: 22 (Fairhaven house), 1930 (rubber hose ad room), 1944 (noir street), 127 (blue mushroom forest), 1031 (rocky island), 9 (indoor pool), 10 (this short, the subway platform). When he steps through a door, it disappears behind him, so he cannot go back the way he came. Time only jumps where the tape cuts to static.

Why this short matters to his arc
Across the previous shorts he has been reactive: he finds exits other people built and he stumbles on his own power. In this short he stops waiting. The beats of his transformation, in order: procedure and hope, then panic, then exhaustion, then resentment, then resolve, then mastery. He starts by addressing an evaluator who does not exist, and ends by creating a door without any help. The glyph realization (it is printed everywhere as an "out" icon) gives him the idea. The failure of the ring on the platform (it does not open a door) teaches him he never needed the nine objects. He is the tenth, and the ring is only a frame. Camera steadiness reverses with his emotional state: shakiest when he is nervous, steadiest when he has purpose.
1. Purpose of the camera
The subject-view capture camcorder is bound to Upload 003's file, issued for orientation exercises. It cannot be switched off and its battery never drains. Procedure says a subject narrates a log into it, so he keeps doing it, long after anyone could be listening. His speech drifts from formal ("Upload 003, logging") to personal ("I") as hope for an evaluator dies. The audience should initially understand that this is just a subject's capture camera, then notice it is the only thing he talks to.
2. Story, in acts (target 2:50)
Act 1: January 3, 2026 (0:00 to 1:15). Arrival, the glyph, the failed ring
* Tape rolls in with the HUD already showing the date (JAN 03 2026), REC dot, time. He is already standing on an empty subway platform. No title card.
* He gives his log in a flat, practiced tone, then turns around. Where the entry door should be is plain tiled wall. This is the second time in a row. He panics briefly and says he cannot be stuck here for another two months (the pool took 64 days). He steadies himself with procedure: observe, then move.
* Everything looks normal and slightly too clean: glazed tile, tactile yellow edge strip, benches, empty ad frames, a departure board reading TRAIN APPROACHING with a soft chirp that repeats. He waits. The train never comes.
* He walks to the exit signs. The EXIT icon is ten dots in a ring. He recognizes it from the extraction sign in the test room. He then notices it on the turnstiles, floor tiles, the stair rail, everywhere: the glyph is printed all over this Build as the standard "out" icon.
* He gathers nine identical yellow wet-floor signs from around the platform (use tracking tears between pickups to compress time), counts them aloud, sets them evenly in a ring on the platform with the gap facing the camera, and steps into the gap as the tenth position.
* The signs rotate, spin erratically and sink into the tile. Two-note chime. Red glow from his hands and the platform lights flush red. Then the red fades. No door appears. He says "Nothing."
* Cut to static.
Act 2: January 29, 2026, day 26 (1:15 to 1:45). Panic and the tunnels
* Platform is now scuffed: nine faint circular seams in the tile where the signs sank, evenly spaced in a ring, gap facing the camera. He walks past them and doesn't look down.
* He jumps down onto the track bed and walks the tunnel. Emergency lighting, dripping water (use the better water drop sound from Open Ocean), cross passages every few dozen meters, each with an EXIT sign carrying the ten-dot ring. Every cross passage leads back to the same platform. The Build is a closed loop.
* He calls out. No answer. His voice echoes oddly (dry, then suddenly very close). His breathing speeds up. He says they must know he is here.
* Cut to static.
Act 3: February 21, 2026, day 49 (1:45 to 2:15). Anger
* Fewer words, slower walking. He sits on a bench, then stands. The shaking in the camera has been replaced by tightness.
* He states the facts: eight months, nobody opened his file. He did everything right. His anger is quiet and controlled, not shouted. He palms the EXIT glyph on the wall and the lights stutter. He says the revenge line.
* Cut to static.
Act 4: March 16, 2026, day 72 (2:15 to 2:52). The hands, the Staging Plane, the hotel
* Platform again. He walks to the nine seams, stands in the gap, and doesn't collect any objects. He says he doesn't need the nine, and never did.
* He lifts both hands, thumbs and index fingers forming a rectangle at arm's length. The chime sounds once, quiet. The air inside the rectangle tears open into a bright white door-shaped hole in the tile wall. This is the first door he has made himself.
* He steps through into the Staging Plane: blank white, no horizon, soft shadowless light. Many door-shaped holes in the distance. The parallax effect must be visible as he turns (see Section 4). He says that every door is his now.
* He walks to the door leading back to the Hotel. The doorway is a normal door. He steps through. The hotel corridor is warm, quiet, patterned carpet. The door he came through, plate 10, shuts behind him. Hold 2 seconds, then cut to static.

4. Materials and rendering
* Frame: 4:3 pillarboxed in 1920x1080. Render 3D at 720x540 and let the VHS pass upscale. 30 fps.
* Platform: a single long island platform with two track beds. Glazed white subway tile on the walls (visible grout, slight yellowing), concrete floor with tactile yellow warning strip along the edge, steel benches, trash bins, a stopped analog clock, empty advertising frames (no real brands, plain gradient posters only), a departure board reading TRAIN APPROACHING, turnstiles and a stair with no upper exit (it ends at a tile wall with an EXIT sign). Fluorescent panels with slight, irregular flicker. Plain, slightly worn, a little too clean.
* Tunnel: dark track bed with ballast, rails with rust, cable runs, emergency lights every 20 m, cross passages with EXIT signs. Water drips. Every cross passage returns to the identical platform.
* Glyph on everything: the ten-dot ring on EXIT signs, turnstile faceplates, stair rails, floor decals. Keep it small and consistent. Official sign shows ten dots. His ring shows nine objects and a gap.
* Wet-floor signs: yellow plastic, scattered around the platform in the first act (spawn eleven, he uses nine).
* Continuity detail: a small label in the corner of the departure board reads 003 / HANDSHAKE: PENDING and never changes during the short.
* Hat visible only in a reflection: one glass-fronted info case on the platform briefly shows the silhouette of a man in a top hat holding a camcorder at chest height, face never clear. Do this once, in Act 2.
* Staging Plane: white void with no visible floor line. Many door-shaped holes spread out, each framed and tagged with a plate. Each doorway is a live portal: render a second scene through each opening using a virtual camera positioned at the player's offset from the door, so the view inside shifts correctly as the camera moves (true parallax, not a flat texture). The doors have a faint red edge glow. The HOTEL door is a normal framed door, tagged HOTEL.
* Hotel corridor (final 4 seconds): use the carpet pattern the creator provides, embedded as a texture. If none is supplied, use a plausible dark patterned carpet as a placeholder and flag it. Warm sconces, closed doors with brass plates, the plate on the door behind him reads 10.
* Red state: deep, dark, bruised red, not alarm red. It stains surface colors (white tile goes brick-pink, yellow signs go orange-brown) instead of acting as a flat overlay. Drive emissive high enough to cross the bloom threshold.
* Door made by hands: the rectangle appears as a clean, flat white door-shaped hole cut into the wall, edges slightly glowing red. A soft hum and slight air pull. No particles, no lightning.
* VHS camcorder pass: low-frequency line jitter (0.25 px max), head-switching band, tracking tears on demand, barrel and vignette on image but not on the OSD, luma blur with ringing, chroma smear, bloom, drifting white balance, AGC noise in darks, dropouts, lifted blacks. No scanlines and no RGB mask.
* OSD: bitmap font with black outline. REC dot, battery (always full), time and date. One small tag: 003. The date changes only at tape breaks.

7. Motion quality
* One continuous handheld take. Cuts only at tape breaks (static) and the final cut.
* Hold at chest height, slightly low and wide, never eye level. Looking down shows only shoes and floor.
* Handheld = walking gait + breathing + tremor driven by a fear curve. The curve inverts his usual pattern:

    * Act 2: jittery, shorter steps, frequent glances back

* Autofocus hunts on the tile and tunnel (blur, lock, slip, lock). Auto-exposure lags about 0.45 s after the red flushes.
* The hands-door: both hands enter frame and hold still. Tilt up slightly as the white hole opens. Do not shake.
* In the Staging Plane, rotate the camera slowly so the parallax in each door is readable.

12. Pitfalls
* Do not show a monster, silhouette other than the one reflection, gore, or jump scares.
* Do not let the ring in Act 1 open a door; the failure is the point.
* Do not show him entering the platform, and do not show him mid-glyph in Act 4. He just stands and lifts his hands.
* Do not explain the CSN, the Company or the hat in dialogue.
* Keep the early platform genuinely mundane before the red appears.
* Pure red will not bloom at low emissive; drive it higher.
* Avoid constant weirdness; let it build, then settle.
* Keep the dialogue dry; heavy reverb ruins intelligibility.
* Do not compress a finished timeline; keep keyframes in original time.
* Clean CG reads as a game; keep the tape pass heavy and the textures plain but believable.
13. Variation space
Fixed: location (empty subway platform and tunnels, Door 10), date range (Jan 3 to Mar 16, 2026), the ten-dot EXIT sign, the failed ring, the revenge line, the hands-door, the Staging Plane, ending in the hotel corridor. Open to your taste: exact tunnel layout, number of cross passages, platform props besides the wet-floor signs, small continuity details, and the one-frame overlay at the final static.
14. Realism
Footage that looks captured, not rendered. When anything conflicts, choose the less polished, less cinematic option. The camera is a 1990s consumer camcorder with a small CCD sensor and electronic stabilization that sometimes overcorrects. Add rolling-shutter skew on fast turns, motion blur only on fast pans, crushed blacks, noisy shadows and slow exposure recovery. Vary incidental details (stains, scuffs, litter, grout wear) even though the platform is modular. No steadicam smoothness, no mathematically perfect lighting, no clean reveals.

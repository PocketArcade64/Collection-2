1. Purpose of the camera
A technician is performing a manual pre-release readiness pass on the castle Build. The automated checker can confirm that geometry and lighting loaded, but it cannot confirm that a hidden-state interaction (the concealed bookcase that opens onto the treasury route) triggers correctly, or that the sun stone asset is present behind it. A human must walk the route, open it, and look. The camcorder is the company's required visual verification tool for manual checks, because the External Monitor does not log interaction state.
The technician is calm, a little tired, procedural. He moves checkpoint to checkpoint. The connection between checkpoints is not walkable, so the tape cuts to static between zones (framed in-world as a checkpoint hop, a normal part of the procedure).

4. Materials and rendering
* Frame: 4:3 pillarboxed in 1920x1080. Render the 3D at low resolution (about 720x540) and let the VHS pass upscale.
* Aim for the world to look believable and realistic when seen through a grainy camcorder filter. Ensure enough important objects have textures. Find HD textures online (stone, carpet, wood, iron, leather book spines, fabric) where applicable and downsample them as needed. All assets must be embedded in the single HTML file (see section 11).
* If anything reads as computer generated, the technician mutters (line 5) that textures and models are still in early development.
* Match the reference photos for architecture, stone, carpet, torches, bookcase, books and sun stone.
* Well-lit zones: stone walls, red carpet runner, tall windows, soft daylight, slightly flat sky.
* Dim zones: stone walls lit only by wall torches (flickering warm light, deep falloff, black between pools of light).
* Bookcase: heavy wooden shelves, many books, one trigger book with a slightly worn spine. The bookcase swings or slides on a visible hinge or track and grinds against stone.
* Passage and treasury: narrow stone passage, then the treasury chamber. A circular stone pedestal holds the sun stone in a golden lattice, with four recesses around it carrying sigils (spiked helmet, two antlers, mushroom, single eye). Each recess glows softly. The sun stone gives the strongest warm light in the short.
* Imperfect world: dust motes in window light, uneven stone edges, worn carpet, wax drips under torches, slight asymmetry. Avoid CG-smooth surfaces.
* VHS camcorder pass: line jitter (0.25 px or less), head-switching band, tracking tears on demand, barrel and vignette on the image but not the OSD, luma blur with edge ringing, chroma smeared right, bloom, drifting white balance, AGC noise in the darks, dropouts, lifted blacks. No scanlines, no RGB mask.
* OSD: bitmap font with black outline. REC dot, battery, time and date (2026-03-01), a small label such as READINESS, a build tag (BUILD 0.9), a checkpoint tag (CP 1 to CP 4), and an iteration counter (ITER 02). Title generator uses the same font, larger.

Subtitles:

6. Motion quality
* One continuous handheld take at chest height, slightly low and wide, never at eye level. Cuts happen only where the tape breaks: roll-in, the checkpoint static hops, and the final static.
* Walking gait (head bob, sway, small roll) plus breathing plus a little tremor. Calm and steady throughout, tightening only slightly at the duplicate bracket. Freeze all motion for the held beat on the bracket.
* Autofocus hunts on dark stone and in the torch corridor: blur, lock, slip, lock. Exposure lags about 0.45 s after sudden light changes, especially stepping from the bright corridor to the dim one and from the dim passage to the glowing sun stone.
* Looking down shows only shoe toes and floor.
* Rolling-shutter skew on fast turns. Motion blur only on fast pans.
* Static cuts are short (about 0.4 to 0.8 s), each ending with the OSD checkpoint tag changed.

10. Pitfalls
* Do not make the castle ominous early. It is a bright, nice, boring inspection.
* Do not use a monster, silhouette, body, glowing eyes or any reveal.
* The bracket must be subtle. If a viewer notices it on first watch, the angle is fine. If it is obvious, reduce it.
* Do not push the screen to red.
* Dim torch zones must stay legible through the VHS pass; lift the shadows slightly so stone texture reads.
* Clean CG interiors read as a game: keep the tape pass heavy and textures plain but believable.
* Do not time-compress a finished timeline; keep keyframes in original time and map through one warp function.
* Keep line wobble at 0.25 px or less and leave the caption box untouched.

11. Engine and build requirements

Suggested modules (inside the one file):

13. Realism
The goal is footage that looks captured, not rendered. When anything conflicts with realism, choose the less polished, less cinematic option.
* 1990s consumer camcorder, small CCD sensor, electronic stabilisation that occasionally overcorrects.
* Autofocus hunts on low-contrast surfaces. Exposure overshoots, then settles.
* Physically grounded light: window light in the bright zones, torches as the real sources in the dim zones, the sun stone as a warm practical source in the treasury.
* The technician speaks like a person: small hesitations, a throat clear, a self-correction ("Bracket five... no, six").
* Breathing and footsteps audibly match the camera bob.
* No dramatic cinematic moves, impossible angles or steadicam smoothness. Nothing is ever resolved into a shape.
* Reference calibration: real estate walkthrough footage on a 1990s camcorder, low-light handheld footage: crushed blacks, noisy shadows, blooming highlights, slow exposure recovery.

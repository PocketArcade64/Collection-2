1. Universe context (you know nothing yet, so read this)
The company is an in-universe corporation that uses the Consciousness Simulation Network (CSN) to copy a person's consciousness into simulated environments to train and evaluate it. The physical person is the Source Subject. The simulated copy is a Cognitive Instance (CI) and is never called a person. Environments are called Builds and carry version numbers. A Technician is a CI doing in-sim work, usually the person holding the camera. The External Monitoris the company's built-in observation of a run from outside. A Delta is the experience a CI gains, compressed so it can be written back into the Source Subject. A Seam (official term: transition boundary) is the boundary between two environments or rooms. Extraction is forcibly pulling a CI out mid-run. A Closed File is a subject marked complete with no extraction.
The company is not a cartoon villain. Staff are calm, professional and matter-of-fact. The horror is in what the procedure implies, never in the tone.
The Closed File rule. A CI that runs unmonitored for too long cannot be evaluated. The company cannot see how it develops or responds to scenarios, so its experience cannot be validated. Unvalidated experience can never be written back as a Delta, because writing it into a Source Subject would put unverified data into a real person. So the file closes automatically and cannot be reopened. To the company this is routine data hygiene. To the Hat Man it is a decision that he does not count.
The Hat Man. In June 2025, Upload 003 was one of the first three consciousness uploads. The scenario was a simple button puzzle. The connection dropped mid-session and the extraction protocol had no handling for a copy that stopped responding. The original body woke up fine. The file was marked closed and the copy was never pulled out. Left running with no evaluator and no objective, the copy learned the simulation's edges by walking them, learned its seams, resets and monitoring blind spots, and began to manipulate it. The top hat is the one detail he kept from his intake photo. He imitates a consciousness because it is the form he began in. He blends in with other copies and moves between simulations through seams. What he wants is to leave and shut down the thing that kept him in it.
Release order matters. The technician shorts release before the hotel arc and the finale, so the audience does not yet know his origin story, the staging plane or the red-glow power. In this short he must be intriguing and human, not explained. Do not state his backstory beyond what the dialogue below says.

3. The Hat Man's character arc: what this short must do
Where he has been. For more than two years he has been abandoned, then furious, then patient. In the earlier technician shorts he works through effects, intrusions and observation. He never speaks to staff.
The injustice he learns here. Until now he could believe he was the victim of a malfunction. A malfunction can be waited out, and nobody can truly hate one. In this short a technician tells him, kindly and in procedural language, that it is a rule: his file closed automatically because he went unmonitored, and it can never be reopened. The company did not fail to save him. It decided long ago that his experience does not count. That is the moment his patience becomes a grievance with a target.
What changes here. He chooses to be seen, and he chooses where. He has watched the control room and knows the External Monitor sees every room except its blind spots. He lures a technician out of camera coverage by telling him to follow the blinking lights, because a conversation in a blind spot is one the company cannot hear. He asks for nothing complicated. He gets the rule, and then a polite apology.
His beats in this short.
1. The lure: a distant voice and a path of blinking overhead lights.
2. The reveal: he is already standing there under a dead camera, calm and still.
3. The question: he says how long he has been inside and gives his number.
4. The rule: the technician explains why the file cannot be reopened.
5. The protest: "That's not fair." He still feels like his Source Subject, has all his memories, and is trapped.
6. The dismissal: "I'm sorry. There's nothing I can do."
7. The answer: he says nothing more. The lights turn dark red and he walks away into the shadows. A light fixture falls on the technician. We never see him cause it.
The technician. He is not cruel. He is the company's calm, professional face, so his apology is sincere and useless. The audience should like him a little.
What the audience must feel.
1. He is a person. His voice is tired, polite, quiet and specific. He is not a monster.
2. His situation is real and sympathetic: more than two years, never extracted, a closed file that cannot be reopened.
3. Something is still wrong: the radio goes silent, the lights lead the technician away, the cameras are dark where they meet.
4. The ending is ambiguous. Did he do it, or did the room? He walked away before it fell. His plan stays unstated: he never says he wants to shut down the CSN.
What he must not do.
* No threats, no raised voice, no approach, no touching the technician. He stays across the field.
* He never raises his hands or glows. Do not show where the red comes from.
* No explanation of the ring, the infection, the staging plane, the red power or the hat.
* Do not state "destroy the CSN" or any equivalent.
* He is not a jump scare. He is already standing there when the camera finds him.
What it sets up. He now has a reason, a target, and proof that the company can hear a technician but not him. After this, he stops asking.
What the audience gets out of it. After the hotel arc and finale, a rewatch reveals that he already knew the rule was permanent when he stood in this field, and that the line he whispers in the finale, "they fixed it for the next one," is the same grievance returning.
4. Purpose of the camera
The technician is doing a render check on Build 6.03, a team-coordination training environment with three sports courts. He needs to see how each court type renders in the build: surface texture, line paint, lighting and scale. He starts in the tennis court, walks through it into the basketball court, then into the large football field. The External Monitor logs frame statistics and positions, but it cannot judge how a surface actually looks to a subject standing on it. Procedure requires first-person visual confirmation, so he carries a camcorder and narrates each court.
The same technician as the data center and cruise ship shorts is the default. He does not recognize the Hat Man at first. If you use the optional line (L16), the two recognize each other from the server hall.
5. Story
The environment is a large indoor sports facility: three huge rooms joined by wide archways, in this order of travel: tennis court, basketball court, then a football field that is a full 100 yards long. Overhead high-bay lighting, translucent roof panels, bleachers, scoreboards, banners with fictional text. It is empty. It is clean and quiet like a closed building. The first two rooms are mundane. The football field is liminal: too big, too quiet, the same lights repeating into haze.
Act 1: Check-in and tennis court (0:00 to 0:38). Tape roll-in with title text (scenario name redacted, date). He explains the render check and why he is recording. He walks the tennis court, noting the surface and the lines, and checks the first seam.
Act 2: Basketball court (0:38 to 1:05). Hardwood court, two hoops, a rack of balls, scoreboard off. A basketball rolls slowly across the floor from nowhere in particular, the familiar trope. Nobody is there. He checks the roster: one CI in the build, himself.

The low hum drops away. The monitor link icon on the camcorder display goes out.
Act 4: The lure (1:30 to 2:08). A calm voice from far away says, "Follow the lights." Overhead lights blink in sequence along a line down the field, one after another, like a path. The technician notices the dome cameras on the ceiling have gone dark past a certain point. That is a coverage gap, which is a defect he is paid to document. He follows the lights, with a brief tape tear covering the long walk.
Act 5: The blind spot (2:08 to 3:01). At the far end, under a dead dome camera, a man in a top hat stands in a single pool of light. He does not move. The technician addresses him. The man says he has been inside more than two years, gives his number, and the technician checks it. The file is closed with no extraction date and cannot be reopened. The man asks why. The technician explains the monitoring rule. The man says it is not fair: he still feels like his Source Subject, has all his memories, and is trapped. The technician says he is sorry and there is nothing he can do.
Ending (3:01 to 3:20). The man says nothing. All the lights turn bright red. He turns and walks away into the shadows, unhurried. The technician looks up. A large light fixture falls toward him. Cut to static at the moment of contact. Two seconds of static, then a one-frame overlay: simulation ID, iteration +1, and the line UPLOAD 003: CLOSED / EXTRACTION: NONE.
The impact is never shown. We never see what the Hat Man did, only that he walked away before it fell.

7.3 Materials and rendering
* Frame: 4:3 pillarboxed in 1920x1080. Render the 3D at low resolution (for example 720x540) and let the VHS pass upscale.
* Aim for worlds that look believable and realistic through the grainy filter. Give important surfaces (hard court, hardwood, turf, court paint, fence mesh, bleachers, padded walls) real texture detail. If the scenery reads as computer generated, the technician comments once that textures and models are still in early development (L05).
* Tennis room (first): hard-court surface with lines, net, an umpire chair, perimeter fence, a ball hopper, high windows letting in flat daylight. Mundane and clean.
* Basketball room (second): maple hardwood with painted lines, two hoops with backboards, a ball rack, retracted bleachers along one side, an unlit scoreboard, wall padding, a few water bottles on a bench. One basketball rolls across the court during the film.
* Football field room (third, the liminal space): a full 100-yard field with end zones, artificial turf with yard lines and numbers, goalposts at each far end, hanging safety netting, a long run of retracted bleachers, padded walls, blocking sleds and pylons. The ceiling is very high, with long rows of identical hanging high-bay fixtures that recede into haze, so the far end is barely visible. Add light atmospheric haze for depth. It must feel too big and too quiet.
* Seams: two wide archways, tennis to basketball and basketball to football. The seam geometry must look slightly too clean: panel lines that continue exactly across the threshold, floor materials that meet without a transition strip.
* Ceiling dome cameras: small dark domes at regular intervals along the ceiling, each with a tiny green status LED. They are the External Monitor's eyes. From a certain point down the football field they are dark, with no LED. The Hat Man stands under one of the dead domes.
* The fixture: one very large industrial high-bay fixture hangs on chains over the spot where the technician stops, in the blind spot. Plant it early so it reads as part of the row. Before the fall, add a faint chain creak and falling dust. It falls when the technician tilts the camera up.
* Lighting states: normal (cold white high-bay light, soft daylight spill); flicker (far fixtures first, spreading toward the camera); blink path (individual fixtures pulse in sequence along a line down the field); dead zone (far fixtures dark except one pool of light over the Hat Man); dark red (every fixture turns deep red at once and the room goes heavy and dim).
* The Hat Man: an ordinary-looking man of average build in plain dark clothes (jacket, shirt, slacks) and a black top hat. He stands still, hands at his sides, in one pool of light. He is visible under good light, but autofocus slips and hunts so his face is never fully resolved: the brim shadows his eyes and the camcorder softens him. He speaks without moving. He turns and walks away only after the lights go red. He never raises his hands or glows. Never show anything monstrous or distorted.
* VHS camcorder pass: small line jitter (0.25 px or less, low frequency), head-switching band, tracking tears on demand, barrel and vignette on the image but not the OSD, luma blur with edge ringing, chroma smeared right, light smear and bloom, drifting white balance, AGC noise in darks, dropouts, lifted blacks, soft clip. Noise at tape rate (30 fps). No scanlines, no RGB mask.
* OSD: bitmap font with black outline. REC dot, battery, time and date, a small label (RENDER CHECK), a build tag (BUILD 6.03), a room tag that updates (TENNIS, BASKETBALL, FOOTBALL), a small monitor link icon (MON) that goes out at 1:29 and stays out, and an iteration counter (ITER 01). The title generator uses the same font, larger.
7.4 Color logic
* Overall: cool white high-bay light, pale gray concrete, muted green turf, warm honey maple, blue-green tennis court. Pleasant and slightly too perfect.
* The Hat Man: dark clothes in a single pool of white light, which is why he reads clearly.
* Red: the one saturated signal, now used for the whole room at the end. Deep, dark and bruised, never alarm red. Drive its emissive high enough to cross the bloom threshold, since dark red has low luminance. Surfaces go near-black red. The room feels heavy rather than bright.
* White balance drifts, especially toward magenta in the red state. Colour is never graded clean.
* Example palette: concrete #b9bdbf, turf #4f6e48, maple #c79c63, tennis blue #3a5f78, tennis green #4a6b4f, light #e7f0f6, hat and jacket #14130f, red #8a0f1c, deep red #4a0610.
7.5 Type and subtitles
* Closed captions (CEA-608 style): monospace (for example IBM Plex Mono 600) about 42 px, white on a solid black box per row, 32 characters or fewer, centred inside the 4:3 frame above the OSD. Untouched by tape noise. Hold for at least max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s). The long explanation (L22) is split into rows that follow the speech.
* Speakers: the technician needs no mark. The Hat Man's lines are captioned with a plain dash prefix. SDH descriptions in brackets: [FOOTSTEPS], [BALL BOUNCING], [LIGHTS BUZZING], [LIGHTS CLICKING], [NO RESPONSE], [SILENCE], [CHAIN CREAKING], [TAPE NOISE].
* The title is camcorder title-generator text over the first shot: scenario name (redacted) and date.
7.6 Motion quality
* One continuous handheld take. Cuts happen only where the tape breaks: power-on roll-in, a tracking tear on the long walk, and the final static.
* Handheld: walking gait (head bob a few cm, sway, small roll), breathing (about 0.27 Hz) and a tremor whose amplitude follows a fear curve. Calm and steady through the first two courts, tightening when the lights flicker and the radio fails, held and still at the sight of the Hat Man, and slowly rising through the conversation. Freeze all of it at a held breath.
* Hold the camera at chest height, slightly low and wide, never eye level. Looking down shows only shoe toes and floor surface. The only time it tilts up is at the end.
* Autofocus is animated with overshoot: blur, lock, slip, lock. It hunts on turf texture and net mesh, and slips when it tries to lock the Hat Man's face.
* Auto-exposure lags about 0.45 s after sudden lighting changes, with gain noise pumping. This matters at the flicker and at the red.
* Electronic image stabilization that occasionally overcorrects. Rolling-shutter skew on fast turns.
* The ending: when the technician looks up, the camera tilts to the fixture, which fills the frame as it falls. It does not flinch away. Cut to static at the moment of contact.

7.9 Native moves
* Autofocus and exposure hunting: the Hat Man resolves only partially, and the red lights cause a slow exposure recovery.
* The OSD as evidence: time code and battery tick regardless. The room tag updates at each seam. The monitor link icon goes out and stays out. The iteration counter changes between the first and last frames.
* Emptiness and repetition: the same court lines, the same banners, the same lights repeating into haze.
* Lighting as plot: normal, flicker, blink path, dead zone and red carry the structure.
* Procedure as dread: he keeps logging and checking while the space stops making sense.
7.10 Pitfalls
* Do not over-weird the first minute. It must feel mundane.
* Do not make the Hat Man tall, thin, glowing, distorted or otherwise monstrous. He is an ordinary man in a hat.
* Do not have him approach, raise his hands, speak while moving, or touch the technician.
* Do not show him doing anything to the fixture. He walks away, and then it falls.
* Do not show the impact, a body, blood or any aftermath. Cut to static at contact.
* Do not light him from below or add a stinger.
* The rolling ball must not be telegraphed. It rolls on the real physics of a nearly level floor.
* The fixture's fall must be physically plausible: chains snap, the fixture swings and drops. Plant it early.
* Clean CG interiors read as a game. Keep the tape pass heavy and the textures plain but believable.
* Line wobble skews text. Keep it 0.25 px or less and leave the caption box untouched.
* Do not time-compress a finished timeline. Keep keyframes in original time and map through one warp function.
7.11 Realism
The goal is footage that looks captured, not rendered. When anything conflicts with realism, choose the less polished, less cinematic option. The camera is a 1990s consumer camcorder with a small CCD sensor. Use motion blur only on fast pans. No steadicam smoothness, no impossible angles, no mathematically perfect lighting, no uniform noise, no clean reveals. Speech feels unscripted: small hesitations, a throat clear, a breath before a number, a self-correction. Breathing and footsteps match the camera bob. Approximate the look of low-light handheld camcorder footage: crushed blacks, noisy shadows, blooming highlights and slow exposure recovery.

9. Delivery requirements
1. One HTML file. Everything (code, libraries, textures, audio, fonts) is contained in that single file so it works on its own on another computer. Load three.js and any libraries from npm or a CDN at build time and inline them. Embed images and audio as data URIs.

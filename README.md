0. What you are building
A single self-contained HTML file that plays a 2:40 to 2:55 first-person found-footage film, rendered live in the browser (three.js), seen through a 1990s consumer camcorder. It must work when the one file is emailed to a different computer. If reference images of the forest are included with this brief, treat them as authoritative for tree species, scale, color and mood. If none are included, use the description below and say so in your final message.
Genre reference (grammar only): the "pages pinned to trees, flashlight, something watching from far away" style of woodland stalking horror. Never use the name of any existing game, creature, film or franchise inside the film, and never copy a recognizable creature design. The figure here is an ordinary silhouette of a man in a hat.
1. Universe context (you know nothing yet, so read this)
The company is an in-universe corporation that uses the Consciousness Simulation Network (CSN) to copy a person's consciousness into simulated environments to train and evaluate it. The physical person is the Source Subject. The simulated copy is a Cognitive Instance (CI) and is never called a person. Environments are called Builds and carry version numbers. A Technician is a CI doing in-sim work, usually the person holding the camera. The External Monitoris the company's built-in observation of a run from outside. Extraction is forcibly pulling a CI out mid-run. A Closed File is a subject marked complete with no extraction.
The company is not a cartoon villain. Staff are calm, professional and matter-of-fact. The horror is in what the procedure implies, never in the tone.
The Hat Man. In June 2025, Upload 003 was one of the first three consciousness uploads. His connection dropped and he was never extracted. His file closed automatically and cannot be reopened. Left running with no evaluator, he learned the simulation's edges, seams and monitoring blind spots, and began to manipulate it. He wears a top hat, the one detail he kept from his intake photo. He wants to leave and to shut down the thing that kept him in it. He is the threat across all these shorts.
Red. Whenever he is near, light in the simulation turns a deep, dark, bruised red. The audience learns this across the shorts. Do not explain it here.
The owl. A blue owl painting hangs on the wall of every house in the Model Estate short (Build 1.3). It is a recurring image that later shorts will explain. Do not explain it here.
Release order matters. The technician shorts release before the hotel arc and the finale. The audience does not yet know his origin, the staging plane or why the owl matters. This short is purely atmosphere and dread.

3. Purpose of the camera
The technician is doing a tree placement audit in the forest section of a dark fantasy Build, in its night variant. The automated checker reports that the forest has loaded but cannot confirm where each tree stands relative to the terrain. Procedure requires a manual first-person pass to confirm that trees sit on the ground and collide correctly. The forest is dark, so he carries a flashlight in his left hand and the camcorder in his right.
The pages on the trees are not part of his task. He logs them as unplaced assets, and that becomes harder to believe as the walk continues.
The technician is a new voice (not the technician from the data center, cruise ship and sports facility shorts). Keep him professional, a little tired, and increasingly careful.
4. Story
The environment is a dense, dark fantasy forest at night: tall old trees, ferns and grass, gentle hills, almost no sky showing through the canopy. It is quiet in the way an empty woods is quiet. It is never still: wind in the canopy, leaves, distant creaks.
Act 1: Check-in and entry (0:00 to 0:30). Tape roll-in with title text (scenario name redacted, date). He explains the audit and why he is recording. He steps into the trees.
Act 2: The first pages (0:30 to 1:10). He walks a line of trees, checking placement. On a trunk is a letter-size page pinned at chest height with a blue owl on it. He logs it as an unplaced asset. There is another on the next tree, then another. Same owl every time.
Act 3: The flashlight (1:10 to 1:30). The flashlight flickers. The camera pans down and to the left to show his left hand gripping it. The beam returns to a steady output. He logs it and continues, a little faster.
Act 4: The sound (1:30 to 1:50). He hears three soft footsteps in the leaves off to his left, then they stop. He swings the beam toward the sound and sees nothing. He turns back around.
Act 5: The red (1:50 to 2:20). A red hue slowly begins to appear. It starts in the shadows at the edge of the beam, then in the canopy glow, until everything is bright red. The beam stays steady and white-ish. He says the lighting state is changing and that he did not request it.
Act 6: The trees sink (2:20 to 2:38). All the trees sink slowly below the ground, trunks and pages together, clipping down through the terrain. What remains is bare grass blades over a gently hilly landscape under a red sky. The wind blows through the blades of grass. Make this shot linger. He says the trees are below grade, all of them.
Ending (2:38 to 2:55). In the far distance, standing on a ridge, a silhouette of a man wearing a hat. He is tiny and still. The technician zooms in, which does not help. He starts to request extraction and the picture cuts to static mid-word.

Nothing chases him. Nothing speaks. We never see the figure's face and never see what happens to the technician.

6. Style prompt: simulation inspection footage
A single recording from a handheld camcorder carried into a dark forest section of a Build to audit tree placement. Never use any existing team, brand, logo, game, creature or film title inside the film. The company, its forms, its marker names, signage and anything unseen are original.
6.1 Essence, and what it is not
* One first-person handheld recording (4:3, VHS-era look), continuous, ending when the tape does.
* A dark, quiet forest that gets one small wrong thing at a time: a page, another page, a sound, a color.
* Dread comes from repetition, a missing explanation, light and darkness, and a professional voice staying professional.
* Not a creature feature: no monster, no gore, no jump-scare stings, no chase, no running. Not a CRT terminal piece (the artefacts are tape and lens, not scanlines).
* The figure is an ordinary silhouette of a man in a hat. He is tiny, still and far away. He never approaches.
* The audience never learns what the pages mean or what the figure wants.

6.3 Materials and rendering
* Frame: 4:3 pillarboxed in 1920x1080. Render the 3D at low resolution (for example 720x540) and let the VHS pass upscale.
* Aim for worlds that look believable and realistic through the grainy filter. Give important surfaces (bark, ground, leaves, grass, the pages) real texture detail. If the scenery reads as computer generated, the technician comments once that textures and models are still in early development (L05).
* Forest: dark fantasy woodland. A mix of tall oak-like and pine-like trees with thick trunks, roots at the base, low branches, and a high dense canopy that blocks the sky. Ferns, undergrowth, fallen leaves, mossy stones, patches of ground fog. Use instanced meshes with levels of detail and keep trees in loose rows so the walk reads as a line. Slightly hilly terrain from a smooth heightmap.
* Dusk quality: the Build is called a dusk Build. Even in the night variant, a faint cool blue glow hangs in the fog and canopy. It must never become bright.
* The pages: each page is 8.5 by 11 inches (21.6 by 27.9 cm), portrait, plain white paper, slightly curled and damp at the edges, pinned at the top with a single tack or two small pieces of tape, mounted at chest height on the trunk facing the path. On each page is the same blue owl painting seen in the Model Estate houses: a stylized front-facing owl in a single blue (#2f5f9a), centred with plain white margins, no text. If the user supplies the owl image, use it exactly. Otherwise draw a simple painted blue owl and keep it identical on every page. Keep the page scale correct against the trunks and the technician's hands.
* The flashlight: a heavy black rubber and metal handheld flashlight held in his left hand. It is a separate rig from the camera: the beam has its own aim, lag and tremor, so when he points it at a sound the beam moves first and the camera follows. Almost the whole film the flashlight and his hand are out of frame. The beam is a warm-white spotlight with a soft cone, visible in the fog.
* The red state: deep, dark, bruised red, never alarm red. It begins as a tint in the shadows at the edge of the beam, then reaches the fog, then the canopy glow, then the ground, then the whole frame. Surfaces change colour as well as emission. The blue owl on the pages goes near-black under red light, not a simple red tint. The beam stays steady and slightly warm.
* The trees sinking: every tree, with its pages still pinned, slides straight down into the ground at a slow even pace, clipping through the terrain surface. No hole, no dust, no broken ground. What remains is the terrain with blades of grass and gentle hills, an open red sky, and thin red ground fog. The sinking is staggered by distance so nearer trees go last.
* The figure: a single man-shaped silhouette on a distant ridge, wearing a hat with a visible brim. Very small in frame, 200 metres or more away, black against the red sky and haze. No face, no detail, no movement except a slight sway. Do not make him tall, thin or unnatural.
* VHS camcorder pass: small line jitter (0.25 px or less, low frequency), head-switching band, tracking tears on demand, barrel and vignette on the image but not the OSD, luma blur with edge ringing, chroma smeared right, light smear and bloom, drifting white balance, AGC noise in darks, dropouts, lifted blacks, soft clip. Noise at tape rate (30 fps). Heavy low-light gain noise in the dark forest. No scanlines, no RGB mask. Do not use a green night-vision look.
* OSD: bitmap font with black outline. REC dot, battery, time and date, a small label (TREE AUDIT), a build tag (BUILD 7.04), and an iteration counter (ITER 01). The title generator uses the same font, larger.
6.4 Color logic
* Forest: near-black green and brown, cold blue-gray fog, faint cool blue canopy glow, warm-white flashlight beam.
* Pages: bright white against dark bark, the only high-contrast objects. The owl is the single blue accent.
* Red: the one saturated signal, and it takes over the whole frame. Drive its emissive high enough to cross the bloom threshold, since dark red has low luminance. Colour is never graded clean.
* White balance drifts, and shifts magenta as the red takes over.
* Example palette: bark #2a231c, moss #2f3a2a, fog #34414d, canopy glow #1b2a3a, beam #f0e3c8, page white #e8e4da, owl blue #2f5f9a, red #8a0f1c, deep red #4a0610.
6.5 Type and subtitles
* Closed captions (CEA-608 style): monospace (for example IBM Plex Mono 600) about 42 px, white on a solid black box per row, 32 characters or fewer, centred inside the 4:3 frame above the OSD. Untouched by tape noise. Hold for at least max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s).
* Speakers: the technician needs no mark. SDH descriptions in brackets: [FOOTSTEPS], [LEAVES RUSTLING], [WIND], [NO RESPONSE], [SILENCE], [TAPE NOISE].
* The title is camcorder title-generator text over the first shot: scenario name (redacted) and date.
6.6 Motion quality
* One continuous handheld take. Cuts happen only where the tape breaks: power-on roll-in and the final static.
* Handheld: walking gait (head bob a few cm, sway, small roll), breathing (about 0.27 Hz) and a tremor whose amplitude follows a fear curve. Careful and even through the first pages, tightening at the flicker, held at the footsteps, shallow and fast during the red, and nearly frozen at the silhouette. Freeze all of it at a held breath.
* Hold the camera at chest height, slightly low and wide, never eye level. Looking down shows only shoe toes, leaves and roots.
* Autofocus is animated with overshoot: blur, lock, slip, lock. It hunts hard on bark and ferns, and it cannot resolve the silhouette.
* Auto-exposure lags about 0.45 s after sudden lighting changes, with gain noise pumping. This matters at the flicker and as the red rises.
* The flicker beat: the flashlight beam stutters off and on. The camera pans down and to the left in a single believable movement to show his left hand holding the flashlight: a bare hand, dark sleeve, knuckles tight on the rubber grip. The beam returns to a steady output. The camera then returns to the path. This is the only time the hand is visible.
* The sound beat: the beam swings left ahead of the camera, finds trees and ferns and nothing else, then they both turn back.
* The silhouette: he lifts the camera and uses the zoom servo for a short, jerky zoom. The picture gets softer and noisier, not clearer.
* The ending: the camera stays at chest height and does not drop.

6.8 Sound palette
* No score. Only what the tape would have recorded.
* Bed: wind in the canopy, soft leaf rustle, distant creaks, a faint electrical hum, almost no insects or birds. The absence of animals is noticeable.
* Footsteps on leaves and dirt, cloth rustle, breath, camcorder clack and motor, zoom servo, AF ticks. The flashlight has a soft click and a faint electrical tick when it flickers.
* The sound beat: three soft footsteps in the leaves, close enough to hear and far enough to place, then stop.
* The red: a low, slow hum that rises as the color spreads, lower in pitch than any lamp hum.
* Held digital-zero moment after "Do you copy?" captioned [NO RESPONSE], then the red hum alone.
* The sinking: a deep, soft grinding rumble, with leaves and bark settling. The pages crinkle once as they go.
* After the trees are gone, the wind drops to almost nothing, leaving only the technician's breath and the red hum.
* Technician voice is dry. Heavy reverb destroys intelligibility.
* Mix: bed about -8 dB under voice, -14 LUFS, no added grain.
6.9 Native moves
* Autofocus and exposure hunting: the geometry resolves only when the camera lets it.
* The OSD as evidence: time code and battery tick regardless. The iteration counter changes between the first and last frames.
* Repetition: the same page, the same owl, on every tree.
* Lighting as plot: dark, beam, red and open sky carry the structure.
* Procedure as dread: he keeps logging while the forest stops making sense.
6.10 Pitfalls
* Do not over-weird the first minute. The audit must feel genuinely mundane before the first page.
* Pages must not be telegraphed with music, glow or close-ups that hold too long.
* Do not show the figure larger, closer, moving toward the camera, or in any detail.
* Do not copy any existing creature. He is a man in a hat.
* Do not add a chase, a scream, a stinger or a jump scare.
* The flashlight must not flicker a second time. It is steady through the red.
* The red must be gradual: at least 25 seconds from first tint to total.
* The trees must sink smoothly, not pop or fade.
* Clean CG interiors read as a game. Keep the tape pass heavy and the textures plain but believable.
* Line wobble skews text. Keep it 0.25 px or less and leave the caption box untouched.
* Do not time-compress a finished timeline. Keep keyframes in original time and map through one warp function.
6.11 Realism
The goal is footage that looks captured, not rendered. When anything conflicts with realism, choose the less polished, less cinematic option. The camera is a 1990s consumer camcorder with a small CCD sensor. Use motion blur only on fast pans. No steadicam smoothness, no impossible angles, no mathematically perfect lighting, no uniform noise, no clean reveals. Speech feels unscripted: small hesitations, a throat clear, a breath before a number, a self-correction. Breathing and footsteps match the camera bob. Approximate the look of low-light handheld camcorder footage: crushed blacks, noisy shadows, blooming highlights and slow exposure recovery.

8. Delivery requirements
1. One HTML file. Everything (code, libraries, textures, audio, fonts) is contained in that single file so it works on its own on another computer. Load three.js and any libraries from npm or a CDN at build time and inline them. Embed images and audio as data URIs.
2. Textures. Find HD textures online where applicable (for example CC0 sources such as Poly Haven or ambientCG for bark, moss, leaf litter, soil, grass and paper). Downsample sensibly and embed them. If your environment cannot reach those sites, use well-made procedural textures and say so clearly in your final message.
3. Dialogue editor mode. Add a toggleable mode (key D and an on-screen button) that shows a simple visual timeline: one track for the technician, each line a draggable block with start time and duration, editable caption text, and Add and Remove buttons. Changes must retime the burned-in captions and the speech audio. Include an Export and Import JSON button for the dialogue so edits can be saved and pasted back into the file. Keep the editor out of exports.
4. Voice. Provide a way to supply recorded voice files per line (ID L01 to L16) or use text-to-speech as a placeholder. Dialogue timing must allow each word proper time to be spoken, with no excess silence unless marked.
5. MP4 export. Provide separate checkboxes to include or exclude Music, Sound Effects and Voice/Narration so each audio element can be exported independently. There is no score in this short, so Music may be disabled but must exist. Each stem exported separately must be exactly the same length as the video MP4, padded with silence where needed.
6. Engine files (as modules within the single HTML).
    * world.js: terrain heightmap, instanced trees with levels of detail, grass blades, ferns, fog, sky, lighting states (night, red), the sinking animation, the distant ridge.
    * pages.js: owl pages, pinning, curl and damp variation, placement on trunks.
    * flashlight.js: the separate beam rig with lag, tremor, flicker and the left-hand visibility pose.
    * figure.js: the distant hat silhouette.
    * tex.js: procedural and embedded textures, the owl image, title text.
    * vhs.js: VHS camcorder ShaderPass.
    * osd.js: bitmap font, REC, battery, clock, TREE AUDIT label, build tag, iteration counter, title generator, final overlay.
    * subs.js: 608 captions, SDH tags.
    * audio.js: wind and leaf bed, footsteps, the three steps, red hum, sinking rumble, voice playback, stem export.
    * story.js: timeline, dialogue table (L01 to L16), camera keyframes, fear and focus curves, flicker beat, sound beat, red ramp, sink sequence, silhouette reveal, ending cut.
    * editor.js: dialogue editor mode.
    * main.js: renderer, post chain (ambient occlusion, depth of field, bloom, then VHS), handheld rig.

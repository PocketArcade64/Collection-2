# Collection-2

Found Footage: ORIENTATION_TEST_003: Build Instructions

Purpose of the camera
Orientation exercises require a subject-view capture, so the evaluator can review how a new subject approaches navigation and problem-solving. The camcorder is session-bound: it stops recording only when the evaluator closes the session. The session was never closed, so the camcorder never stops. That explains the 135-day span and every later short where he is still carrying it.
Dialogue timing
Ensure the proper amount of time is allocated for each word to be spoken. Do not leave excess empty time between sentences if unnecessary. Use discretion to make the dialogue sound like something a human would actually say, spaced like a professional production.
Assets
Use the First-person hands and same sleeves you used in the previous shorts.
Use the hands whenever he presses buttons, taps the dots, or looks at his hands. If the file is not provided, build simple low-poly procedural hands as a fallback.

Story outline
Timings are in the timed script below.
Act 1: June 10, 2025 (the test)
* Tape roll-in with title-generator text: ORIENTATION EXERCISE, SUBJECT 003, JUN 10 2025. (Include this in this short since this is the beginning of the hat man storyline)
* A bare test-facility room. A wall-mounted electronic sign reads 003 / HANDSHAKE: OK.
* A calm Evaluator on the intercom teaches the toggle: pressing red opens every red door and closes every blue door, and blue does the reverse.
* Room 2 holds the exit, a blue door, and a cube. The only button pedestal is in Room 1. Pressing blue opens the blue exit but shuts the red door he needs to walk through, so he fetches the cube, wedges it in the red doorway so it cannot close, and then presses blue. Both colors are open at once.
* If the room reads as computer generated, the Evaluator comments that textures are unfinished in this early test build.
* Mid-session, static takes over the intercom, her last word cut off. The wall sign flips to HANDSHAKE: PENDING. He can't be extracted.
* He waits, then finishes the puzzle anyway, since procedure says to.
* The last room has an EMERGENCY EXTRACTION sign, with ten dots in a ring around the words. He presses the button and nothing happens. Cut to static.
Act 2: August 17, 2025 (the ritual)
* Same test complex, in the final room. It is scuffed. Sixty-eight tally marks are scratched low on one wall, one for each day since June 10. The sign still reads HANDSHAKE: PENDING.
* He taps the ten dots on the extraction sign one at a time with his finger.
* He arranges loose objects (the cube, stools, tubing, pedestal caps) into nine evenly spaced pieces in a ring, and stands in the gap himself as the tenth position. The gap faces the camera.
* The objects spin in place erratically, then sink into the floor. As the last one sinks, a single two-note chime plays. It reads as an error tone, unexpected and not accounted for, even to him.
* He looks down at his hands and a red glow is coming off them. He tests it by turning the lights red, his signature ability. The red lights are silent apart from a very low hum.
* Cut to static.
Act 3: October 23, 2025 (the Staging Plane)
* He bashes at the wall and it glitches. An outline opens into a rectangular white door-shaped hole.
* Beyond it is the Staging Plane, a blank white expanse with many door-shaped holes, each carrying a Build tag.
* He arranges nine objects in a circle there and stands in the gap as the tenth position. The objects spin and sink as before, with the single chime as the last one goes. This time he reaches his hands out as they go under, and a red glow seeps outward from him and into each of the doors in the plane.
* He stops at the door tagged HOTEL and goes through. The short ends as it closes behind him.
Style prompt: Simulation Orientation Footage
A single recording from a handheld subject-view camcorder, issued to a new subject for an orientation exercise in a bare test facility. Genre reference (grammar only): found footage, new-weird, “the system is wrong and nobody knows why” horror; 1990s consumer camcorder footage. Never use any existing organisation, logo, game or film title inside the film. The company, its forms, its marker names and anything unseen are original. Suits films of 2:30 to 2:50.
1. Essence, and what it is not
* One first-person handheld recording (4:3, VHS-era camcorder look), with date jumps that the tape's clock records without comment.
* A routine puzzle exercise that loses contact with the outside, then a man alone teaching himself the rules of the place that kept him, until he walks out of it.
* Dread comes from a calm voice that stops, procedure that continues anyway, and a quiet discovery that he can change the room.
* He is never shown clearly. Only his hands and his boots. He wears no hat, and his face is never visible.
* Not a creature feature (no monster, no gore, no stings). Not menace: the red glow is a discovery, not a villain flourish. Do not explain the ring. Not a CRT terminal piece (artefacts are tape and lens, not scanlines).
2. Materials and rendering
* Frame: 4:3 pillarboxed in 1920×1080. Render the 3D at low resolution (e.g. 720×540) and let the VHS pass upscale.
* Aim for the worlds to look believable and realistic enough when seen through a grainy filter on the camcorder. Ensure that enough important objects have textures and the scenery looks believable when seen through the grainy camcorder filter.
* Test rooms: pale grid-banded walls with thin dark bands, plain floor tiles, slab doors with color-coded frame lights, a plain cube with faint edge wear, stools, tubing, pedestal caps. Early-build look: flat surfaces, simple lights, no clutter. If anything reads as computer generated, the Evaluator remarks that textures are unfinished.
* Layout: Room 1 has the only button pedestal (a red button and a blue button), a red door leading to Room 2, and a blue door beside it that opens onto an empty alcove. Room 2 is a small chamber with the cube in one corner and a blue exit door on the far wall. Room 2 has no buttons. Pressing blue opens every blue door and closes every red door, and pressing red does the reverse. The final room holds the extraction sign.
* Wall-mounted electronic sign: amber segmented characters, “003 / HANDSHAKE: OK” then “HANDSHAKE: PENDING”. It exists only in the test complex.
* Intercom grille mounted high on the wall.
* Extraction sign: ten dots in a ring around the words EMERGENCY EXTRACTION, in a glossy frame, with a large button below.
* Aug 17: scuffed floor, 68 tally marks (13 groups of five plus three) low on one wall.
* Ring: nine objects evenly spaced, one gap at the bottom middle, facing the camera. They spin erratically, accelerate, sink through the floor, and the tile reseals seamlessly.
* Red glow: emissive light from his palms (visible in the first-person hands), staining nearby surfaces. In the plane, it crawls across the floor and soaks into each door frame, leaving a faint ring with a gap that fades almost to nothing.
* Wall glitch: displacement and tearing of the wall surface, resolving into a soft-edged white rectangle.
* Staging Plane: blank white with gentle light falloff and a faint floor reflection so it doesn't read as a missing render. Door-shaped holes with small black tags and white text. One reads HOTEL, and it stands within a few steps of the ring. The plane has no signs or panels.
* VHS camcorder pass: small line jitter (≤ 0.25 px, low-frequency), head-switching band, tracking tears on demand, barrel and vignette on the image but not the OSD, luma blur with edge ringing, chroma smeared right, light smear and bloom, drifting white balance, AGC noise in darks, dropouts, lifted blacks, soft clip. Noise at tape rate (30 fps). No scanlines, no RGB mask.
* OSD: bitmap font with black outline. Default camcorder HUD only: REC dot, battery, time and date. No subject label. The title generator uses the same font, larger.
3. Color logic
* Normal: neutral pale grays and off-whites with amber from the sign.
* Red: the one saturated signal. A deep, bruised red, driven high enough to cross the bloom threshold.
* Staging Plane: blown pale white, slightly cool.
* Door colors: red door and blue door stay the same in every state, tinted by ambient light.
* Example palette: wall #cfd2cf, band #7d8587, floor #9a9d98, red door #8c1f24, blue door #20467a, sign amber #ffb000, red glow #b0141f, plane #f4f4f2.
4. Type and captions
* Closed captions (CEA-608 style): monospace (e.g. IBM Plex Mono 600) ~42 px, white on a solid black box per row, ≤ 32 characters per row (break longer lines into two rows), centred inside the 4:3 frame above the OSD, untouched by tape noise. Hold ≥ max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s).
* Captions come only from the timed script below. 003 needs no speaker mark. The Evaluator is captioned [EVALUATOR]. SDH descriptions: [INTERCOM STATIC], [CHIME], [LOW HUM], [SILENCE].
5. Motion quality
* Handheld at chest height, slightly low and wide. Walking gait, breathing (~0.27 Hz), small tremor that stays low and steady throughout. He is calm.
* Looking down shows his hands and boot toes.
* Autofocus hunts on blank surfaces, especially the white plane, locking only on door frames.
* Auto-exposure lags (~0.45 s) after sudden lighting changes.
* During each ring ritual he stands still with the camera held level on the ring.
6. Camera grammar
* Steady forward walk: routine, the test. Used for the opening rooms.
* Hold on a panel: information. Used for the sign flipping to PENDING.
* Repeated pressing of a button: refusal of the system. Used for the extraction button.
* Slow tilt down to hands: discovery. Used for the red glow.
* Level hold on the ring: ritual. Used for both ring sequences.
* Pan across many doors: scale. Used for the Staging Plane.
* Framing: the audience never gets a clean look at him. Hands and boots only.
7. Sound palette
* No score. Only what the tape would have recorded.
* Rooms: faint fluorescent hum, footsteps, clothing rustle, breath, the camcorder's motor.
* Intercom: clean at first, then static swells, her voice cut mid-syllable, then a long hiss that fades to room tone.
* The chime: a low two-note tone, like an elevator chime played underwater, muffled and slightly detuned. It plays exactly once per ritual, at the moment the last object sinks. It reads as an error alert and is the same sound in every film.
* While the objects spin: a rising mechanical whir and the scrape of tile resealing. No tonal sound until the chime.
* Red lights: silent except for a very low hum that swells with the glow and fades with it. No chime.
* Staging Plane: almost silent. Footsteps with a long soft decay.
* Mix: bed about −8 dB under voice. Master −14 LUFS.
8. Native moves
* The OSD as evidence: the date jumps between acts and the clock keeps running regardless.
* The tally marks as a time counter that matches the date jump.
* The wall sign as the status record, readable at a glance.
* The single chime as a recurring signature that later films reuse.
9. Pitfalls
* A pure white plane looks like a render failure. Keep the falloff and floor reflection.
* Never show his face. No hat appears anywhere in this film.
* The ring must always read as nine objects with the gap toward camera.
* Do not explain the ring or the glow. Do not add stings or score.
* Keep the chime identical every time so it's learnable, and play it only once per ritual.
* The cube must visibly matter: it is the only way both doors can be open at once.
* Pure red never blooms at low emissive; drive it far higher.
10. Engine and export
* world.js: test rooms, doors, button pedestal, wall sign, tally marks, extraction sign.
* plane.js: the Staging Plane, door holes, tags.
* ritual.js: ring placement, erratic spin, sink, red glow spread, wall glitch.
* tex.js: procedural wall panels, tile, scuffs, sign text, tags.
* vhs.js: VHS camcorder ShaderPass.
* osd.js: bitmap font, REC, battery, clock, title generator.
* subs.js: 608 captions, [EVALUATOR] tags.
* story.js: timeline, date jumps, light states, camera keyframes, focus and exposure curves.
* main.js: renderer, post chain (GTAO + DOF + bloom, then VHS), handheld rig.
* Export: when exporting the video as an MP4, provide separate checkboxes to include or exclude Voice/Dialogue, Sound Effects, Music (unused, since there is no score), and Captions, so each can be exported independently.
* Voice is recorded separately. Also export a dialogue cue sheet (CSV) with start time, speaker, line, and max duration in seconds, taken from the timed script, so generated voice can be fitted to the timings.
* The camcorder HUD stays default for every short in the series.
11. Variation space
Fixed: the test complex, the June/August/October structure, the red and blue puzzle and the cube, the wall sign, the extraction sign, the ring ritual, the single chime, the Staging Plane, the HOTEL door, length 2:30 to 2:50. Open: the exact objects in the ring, the Evaluator's exact lines, the layout details of Room 2, whether a tracking tear marks each date jump.
12. Realism
The goal is footage that looks captured, not rendered. When anything conflicts with realism, choose the less polished option. The camera is a 1990s consumer camcorder with a small CCD sensor and electronic stabilization that occasionally overcorrects. Hold it at chest height, with rolling-shutter skew on fast turns. Keep the rooms plain and slightly worn: faint scuffs, uneven shading. Speech feels unscripted, with small hesitations and a breath before a number. No cinematic camera moves, no clean reveals, no steadicam smoothness.

Production notes
* Spoken lines: 31 in all, 25 for 003 and 6 for the Evaluator (5 if the textures line is cut). Most of the runtime is procedure, silence, and the ritual sequences.
* Puzzle logic: pressing blue opens the blue exit door in Room 2 but closes the red door he needs. The cube wedged in the red doorway is what lets both stay open.
* Evaluator's last word: her “appro-” cuts mid-syllable without any change in tone, so it reads as the line dropping and not as emotion.
* Tally count: 68 marks is the exact count of days between June 10 and August 17, so viewers can check it.
* Where the fragments come from: the Act 3 objects are the panel pieces from the wall he bashes. That explains where nine objects come from in a blank plane.
* If the textures line is cut: hold the silence from 0:31 to 0:36 so the runtime holds.
* Series rules set by this film that later shorts reuse: a single two-note chime plays once, when the last object of a ring sinks; red lights are silent with a very low hum; the camcorder HUD is the default (REC, battery, time and date); the wall sign exists only in the test complex.

Each doorway intro/out of the staging plane should work like a window into another 3D world, not a
picture on the wall. When the camera moves or turns, the objects inside
should shift with parallax, so near things move more than far things.
The technical wording is what makes Claude build it correctly:

PORTAL NOTE
- Use portal rendering. Each destination is its own separate scene.
- Every frame, render that scene to a render target from a virtual
  camera whose pose matches the player's pose relative to the door,
  placed at the destination scene's door anchor.
- Draw the doorway plane with a shader that samples the render target
  using SCREEN-SPACE coordinates (gl_FragCoord / resolution), not the
  plane's UVs. This is what creates the depth illusion.
- Use an oblique near-plane clip so nothing from the destination
  appears in front of the door plane.
